\section{The WeBWorK scripts}\label{sec:ref-webwork}

\texttt{webwork-check} and \texttt{webwork-deploy} moved into the machinery on
2026-09-06 and were not documented at the time. This entry is written
retrospectively, so it records what they do \emph{now} rather than how they got
here.

Both take the course repo to be the \emph{parent} of the machinery directory,
overridable with \env{COURSE\_ROOT}, and both refuse to run if that parent has
no \file{.course-machinery/} in it.
\texttt{pg-profile} and \texttt{pg-answer-variation}, at the end of this entry,
take no course at all.

\subsection*{Where a course keeps its set definitions}

Since 2026-09-13 a single-section practice set is curated \emph{inside its topic}, as group directories of symlinks under \file{<topic>/webwork-sets/}, and its \file{.def} is generated from them.
That is safe for a topic repo meant to be reused by a second course, because what it commits is only membership and order; dates, headers and set type are parameters of the generation.
Only sets that draw on several topics --- a gateway, a diagnostic, a review --- still keep a hand-written \file{.def} at \emph{course} level, and both scripts resolve that directory the same way:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l Y@{}}
\env{WEBWORK\_SETS\_DIR} & wins outright if set.\\
\file{WEBWORK/} & the current name, used when the directory exists.\\
\file{\_assessments/webwork-assignments/} & the pre-2026-09-10 name, still
  honoured so a course that has not moved keeps working.\\
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

The rename followed the audience-first scheme the course roots already use
(\file{SYLLABUS/}, \file{TAs/}, \file{ADMIN/}, \file{TESTS/}), which a
lowercase \file{\_assessments/} with a nested second level fitted badly.
\emph{The fallback is temporary}: drop it once every course has moved, and the
lookup collapses back to one line. MATH1003 has moved; MATH3503 has not.

\subsection*{webwork-check}

\begin{manexsource}
webwork-check [--paths] [--drift] [TOPIC ...]
\end{manexsource}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l Y@{}}
\texttt{-{}-paths} & every reference to a problem resolves: each
  \texttt{source\_file} line in a course-level \file{.def} or one under
  \file{webwork-sets/}, and each symlink in a group directory. Finding
  \emph{no} references of either kind is itself a failure --- a check that
  cannot find its input must say so.\\
\texttt{-{}-drift} & every \file{.pg} under a topic's \file{webwork/diffyqs/}
  and \file{webwork/opl/} either matches the clone it was copied from, or
  records the blob it was copied from and is checked against that.\\
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

With no flag, both run.
A \env{TOPIC} operand narrows \texttt{-{}-drift} and the topic half of \texttt{-{}-paths}; the course-level \file{.def} files are always checked, since each names its own topics.

\paragraph{Group directories.}
A group directory is named \file{<section>\_<Title>-<nn>-<Group>} and holds symlinks named \file{NN-originalname.pg}.
In a \emph{settled} one, which is committed, every link must be relative and land inside that topic's own \file{webwork/}.
A \emph{draft} one, named \file{\ldots{}-{}-CANDIDATES-CLAUDE} and gitignored, links into the upstream clones because nothing has been forked yet, so only ``does it resolve'' applies.
Other files, such as a \file{README.md} of notes, are ignored.
The generated \file{webwork-sets/\_for-upload/} is skipped entirely.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l Y@{}}
\texttt{BROKEN} & the link does not reach a file. Any directory.\\
\texttt{NOT FORKED} & a settled link lands outside the topic's
  \file{webwork/} --- in an upstream clone, or in another topic's repo.\\
\texttt{ABSOLUTE} & a settled link is forked but absolute, so it breaks on
  any other machine.\\
\texttt{NOT A LINK} & a real \file{.pg} in a settled directory: the problem
  would exist twice, and the copy escapes the drift check.\\
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Known gaps.}
A server-flat \file{local/\ldots} path with no topic in front of it, as in MATH1003's diagnostic test, reports as \texttt{UNKNOWN SOURCE}.
A gateway's \texttt{group:<name>} pool references are not modelled, so such a \file{.def} cannot pass.

\paragraph{The OPL has two problem trees.}
This is the trap worth knowing. The Open Problem Library clone holds
\file{OpenProblemLibrary/} (the curated library, served at
\file{templates/Library/}) and \file{Contrib/} (contributed collections, served
at \file{templates/Contrib/}) as \emph{siblings}. A single clone variable
pointing at \file{OpenProblemLibrary/} therefore cannot see \file{Contrib/} at
all, which reported every contributed problem as \texttt{GONE} and every
\texttt{source\_file = Contrib/\ldots} line as \texttt{UNKNOWN SOURCE}. Fixed
2026-09-10, when MATH1003 began harvesting the CCCS collection.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l Y@{}}
\env{OPL\_ROOT} & the clone root; moves the two below together.\\
\env{OPL\_CLONE} & \file{OpenProblemLibrary/} within it. Unchanged in meaning,
  so existing overrides still work.\\
\env{OPL\_CONTRIB} & \file{Contrib/} within it.\\
\env{DIFFYQS\_CLONE} & Lebl's \file{diffyqs-webwork} clone.\\
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Provenance is the second path level.}
A topic's \file{webwork/} has one subdirectory per upstream, named for it:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l Y@{}}
\file{opl/} & from \file{OpenProblemLibrary/}, the curated tree.\\
\file{contrib/} & from \file{Contrib/}, its \emph{sibling} upstream --- not
  part of the OPL proper, and not reviewed.\\
\file{diffyqs/} & from Lebl's \file{diffyqs-webwork}.\\
\file{local/} & ours outright.\\
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

Until 2026-09-11 the contributed copies sat under \file{opl/Contrib/}, which
reads as ``the Contrib part of the OPL'' --- a tree that does not exist ---
and which flattened away \file{OpenProblemLibrary/} for one upstream while
keeping \file{Contrib/} for the other. The sibling layout matches the clone,
matches \file{templates/Library} and \file{templates/Contrib} on the server,
and removes the special case drift-checking needed to undo the nesting.

Post-flip \file{.def} paths need no special case either way, being
topic-rooted already.

\paragraph{Drift outcomes.}
\texttt{DIFFERS} (differs from upstream with no blob recorded --- unexpected),
\texttt{MOVED} (upstream changed a file we deliberately modified --- news, not
failure), \texttt{GONE} (upstream deleted or renamed it), \texttt{AHEAD}
(upstream has problems in these sections that we do not, reported as a count).

An edited problem \emph{stays where it is} and records its origin in a header
line:

\begin{manexsource}
# UPSTREAM-BLOB: 02b6a97499f4a05af144116dacfc0835c9bcc0ec
\end{manexsource}

That separates the two questions drift bundles together --- ``have I edited
this?'' and ``has upstream moved?'' --- and keeps the second answerable on
exactly the files where an upstream fix is most expensive to absorb by hand.
Filing edited problems away under \file{local/} would give that up;
\file{local/} is for problems that are ours outright.

\subsection*{webwork-build}

\begin{manexsource}
webwork-build [-n] [-t TEMPLATE] [-s SETID] SECTION
\end{manexsource}

Generates one set, ready to upload, from a section's settled group directories.
\texttt{webwork-build OS1.1} takes every \file{<topic>/webwork-sets/OS1.1\_<Title>-<nn>-<Group>/} in group-number order, and every \file{NN-name.pg} link inside each in \file{NN} order.
Drafts are never included, and \texttt{OS1.1} does not match \texttt{OS1.10}, because the underscore is part of the match.

It writes, under the gitignored \file{<topic>/webwork-sets/\_for-upload/}:

\begin{manexsource}
setOS1.1_Review_of_Functions/00-setHeader.pg
setOS1.1_Review_of_Functions/01-sw4_7_33.pg   ...
setOS1.1_Review_of_Functions/LICENSE.md
setOS1.1_Review_of_Functions/MANIFEST.tsv
setOS1.1_Review_of_Functions.def
setOS1.1_Review_of_Functions.tgz
\end{manexsource}

The problems are flat \emph{copies}, renumbered continuously across groups: if group 01 holds four links, the first problem of group 02 becomes \file{05-}.
The number is what makes flattening safe, since basenames collide across the problem library, and it keeps the reviewed order visible on the server.
Delete a link and build again, and the set renumbers.
Upload the tarball into \file{templates/} and unpack it there; that gives \file{templates/setOS1.1\_\ldots/} beside its \file{.def}, the layout of the \file{setDemo/} webwork2 ships.

\paragraph{The template.}
Dates, the set header, set type and per-problem settings belong to a course and a term, not to a topic, so they come from a template the course keeps: \file{set-template.def.in} in its WeBWorK directory, or whatever \texttt{-t} names.
It is an ordinary \file{.def} with exactly one \texttt{problem\_start}~\ldots~\texttt{problem\_end} block, repeated once per problem, and placeholders filled in anywhere:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l Y@{}}
\texttt{\{SETID\}} & the set ID, \texttt{<SECTION>\_<Title>} unless \texttt{-s} gives one.\\
\texttt{\{SECTION\}}, \texttt{\{TITLE\}} & from the directory names; the title
  with underscores as spaces.\\
\texttt{\{HEADER\}} & the copied set header's path on the server.\\
\texttt{\{PROBLEM\_ID\}}, \texttt{\{SOURCE\_FILE\}} & per problem, in the
  problem block.\\
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

A \texttt{\#! header: PATH} line names the set header, relative to the course repo.
It is copied into the set, so a header kept in one topic serves a set from any other without a cross-topic reference.
\file{SAMPLE-set-template.def.in} in the machinery is the annotated reference.

\paragraph{What it checks.}
Nothing is written unless every link resolves, is relative and lands inside the topic's own \file{webwork/} --- the rules \texttt{webwork-check} applies to a settled directory --- and has an \file{NN-} prefix.
All the groups must come from one topic and share one title, since a mismatch is almost always a typo in a directory name.
After writing, it checks that every file the \file{.def} names is in the set, and it opens its own tarball with Python's \texttt{tarfile} to confirm there are no AppleDouble members, because \texttt{tar tzf} hides them.
It deliberately does not call \texttt{webwork-check}, which also examines every course-level \file{.def} and could fail for reasons unrelated to this set.

\paragraph{Review sets.}
\begin{manexsource}
webwork-build --review [-r ROUND] [-n] [-t TEMPLATE] SECTION
\end{manexsource}

A \emph{draft} group directory, \file{\ldots{}-{}-CANDIDATES-CLAUDE}, holds links to candidates wherever they live, and reviewing one means reading it on the server.
The server already has the upstream trees, so a review set needs no problem files, only a \file{.def}.
\texttt{-{}-review} writes one into each draft directory of the section, as \file{set<draft name>-r<ROUND>.def}, and bundles them flat into \file{\_for-upload/<SECTION>-review-r<ROUND>.tgz}.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l Y@{}}
link reaches & reviewed as\\
\hline
the OPL clone's \file{OpenProblemLibrary/X} & \file{Library/X}\\
the OPL clone's \file{Contrib/X} & \file{Contrib/X}\\
the diffyqs clone's \file{X} & \file{diffyqs-webwork/X}\\
a fork at \file{<topic>/webwork/opl/X}, \ldots & its upstream path, flagged in the \file{.def}: the review shows upstream, not our edit\\
anything else & nothing: listed in the \file{.def}'s header, to be read locally\\
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

The template supplies dates and per-problem settings; its header is replaced by \texttt{defaultHeader}, since nothing is uploaded for it to point at.
Draft links may be absolute, but must resolve and must carry an \file{NN-} prefix, which is the review order.

\emph{The round is part of the set ID}, because an import skips a set name that already exists: after culling links, build \texttt{-r 2}, and the culled round arrives as a new set.
Rebuilding a round replaces the draft's earlier \file{.def} files; old rounds stay on the server until deleted there, which is harmless, since review sets carry no student data.

\paragraph{The manifest.}
\file{MANIFEST.tsv} records each copy's source path and git blob hash, and it travels to the server inside the set directory.
Mid-term fixes happen on the server, so at the end of term the set directory is downloaded and the manifest says which file each copy came from and whether it was edited there.
That merge back into \file{<topic>/webwork/} is load-bearing: a skipped one silently loses the fixes next year.

\subsection*{webwork-deploy}

\begin{manexsource}
webwork-deploy [-o DIR] [-n] [TOPIC ...]
\end{manexsource}

Builds one uploadable \file{.tgz} per topic, to be unpacked inside the
course's \file{templates/} on the server, producing a topic-rooted tree:

\begin{manexsource}
templates/<topic>/opl/Rochester/setAlgebra15Functions/x.pg
templates/<topic>/contrib/CCCS/CollegeAlgebra/3.1/x.pg
templates/<topic>/local/...
templates/<topic>/LICENSE.md
\end{manexsource}

The \file{.def} files go in at the top level of \file{templates/}
separately, and are deliberately \emph{not} in the tarball: the import scan
searches \file{templates/} four levels deep, so a copy shipped inside the
topic tree as well would put two identically named set definitions in front
of it. The script shipped them until 2026-09-11, against what this page
already said.

The tarball root is named for the \emph{topic}, not the repo directory, which
is why it stages into a temporary directory rather than tarring in place.
It still \emph{reports} how many \file{.def} files belong to the topic, so the
count of what to upload separately is not left to memory.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l Y@{}}
\texttt{-o DIR} & write tarballs here (default \file{./\_deploy}, gitignored).\\
\texttt{-n} & dry run: report what would go in, build nothing.\\
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\emph{Upload the tarball before importing the sets.} Post-flip paths point
inside \file{templates/<topic>/}, so importing first leaves every problem in
the set unresolvable.

\texttt{webwork-deploy} runs \texttt{webwork-check} first and refuses to build
if it fails --- shipping a set whose paths do not resolve is the failure mode
this layout exists to prevent.

\subsection*{pg-profile and pg-answer-variation}

Two gates for judging a candidate problem before it goes into a set.
Both render the problem rather than reading its source, because a statement read from a slice of the source hides its later answer blanks exactly where the slice ends.
They moved here from MATH1003's \file{.course-machinery-local/} on 2026-09-13: nothing in them is specific to a course, and a course with an empty overlay could not run them at all.

Both need \texttt{pr-pg-render} on \env{PATH}, which renders a \file{.pg} locally through the pg library with no server.
Both take any mix of \file{.pg} files and directories, searched recursively.

\begin{manexsource}
pg-profile [-n SEEDS] [--no-statement] [--tier1-only] FILE.pg|DIR ...
\end{manexsource}

Everything needed to judge one problem, on one screen: its OPL tags, the macros it loads, every answer blank, whether its checker accepts its own stated answers, a seed sweep, whether the answers vary, the state of its solution, and the full statement.
Findings come in two tiers, and only the first is a veto.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l Y@{}}
Tier 1 & \emph{buggy}: errors on some seeds, a checker that rejects its own
  answer, a macro the pinned pg clone lacks, or a problem whose every blank ---
  a multiple-choice one included --- is the same on every seed. Sets the exit
  status.\\
Tier 2 & \emph{editable}: a video embed, named software, a claimed attempt
  limit, a cosmetic warning, a commented-out solution stub. A reason to fork and
  edit the problem, not to reject it.\\
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

Macros are checked against the pg clone at \env{PG\_ROOT}, the same variable \texttt{pr-pg-render} reads.
A clean profile means the problem \emph{works}, never that it is good: correctness of the answer, clarity, and fit to the course stay human work.

\begin{manexsource}
pg-answer-variation [-n SEEDS] [-q] FILE.pg|DIR ...
\end{manexsource}

The narrower check, for a whole finished selection: do the answers change with the seed?
A problem can redraw its graph and move its coefficients while its correct answer stays a literal, and nothing errors, so only comparing answers across seeds finds it.
A \emph{choice} blank is a drop-down, radio button, checkbox or typed word such as ``even'' or ``None'', since a constant one is guessable.
The verdicts are graded exactly as \texttt{pg-profile} grades them, so the two tools cannot disagree about a problem:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l Y@{}}
\texttt{CONSTANT-CHOICE} & every blank, a choice blank included, never
  changes: a giveaway. Sets the exit status.\\
\texttt{CHOICE-FIXED} & a choice blank never changes while others do. One
  skill goes free, which may or may not matter to the set, so it is reported,
  not failed.\\
\texttt{CONSTANT} & every blank is fixed and none is a choice. Reported; can
  be legitimate.\\
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

Until 2026-09-13 typed words did not count as choices, and any fixed choice failed; the first let a problem answering ``even'' on every seed through, and the second was stricter than \texttt{pg-profile}.
\texttt{-n} defaults to 8 seeds, and \texttt{-q} prints only the problems that fail.
